The most common question we get at Beyond Arenal isn’t *if* you should hike the volcano—it’s *where* to hike it.

With no single "official" entrance that covers everything, visitors are often forced to choose between the **Arenal Volcano National Park**, the **Arenal 1968 Private Reserve**, and the **Arenal Observatory Lodge trails**. Making the wrong choice can mean the difference between an unforgettable volcanic exploration and a crowded, underwhelming walk.

Here is the definitive 2026 guide from our local experts—designed to cut through the noise and help you pick the perfect trail for your style.

## The Big Rule: You Cannot Hike to the Crater
Let’s get the most important safety fact out of the way: **It is illegal and extremely dangerous to hike to the summit of the Arenal Volcano.** 

Since the devastating 1968 eruption and the decades of activity that followed, the upper slopes remain unstable, emitting toxic gases and prone to sudden rockfalls. Anyone promising a "crater hike" is risking your life and operating illegally. All the trails we discuss below safely explore the base, the lava fields, and the surrounding rainforest.

## 1. Arenal 1968 Private Reserve: The Best for Lava & Views

If you want to walk on history, this is your spot. **Arenal 1968** is a private reserve located right next to the National Park, but it offers a much more dramatic experience because the trails go directly over the magnificent lava flow from the 1968 eruption.

- **The Vibe**: Rugged, lunar, and awe-inspiring.
- **The Trails**: The primary "Lava Trail" (Sendero Colada 1968) takes about 2 hours. It winds through regenerating forest before emerging onto the hardened, metallic-looking basaltic rocks.
- **The View**: The observation point here provides what we consider **the best unobstructed view of the volcano's perfect cone** and the sprawling Lake Arenal behind you.
- **Expert Tip**: The dark volcanic rocks radiate heat by mid-day. Start this hike as early as 8:00 AM or go at 3:00 PM for the golden hour.

**Is it for you?** Yes, if you want the most "volcanic" experience and the best photos.

## 2. Arenal Volcano National Park: The Classic Route

The official **National Park** is the most famous option, offering relatively flat, well-maintained trails. 

- **The Vibe**: Family-friendly, educational, and accessible.
- **The Trails**: The *Las Coladas* trail (2km) takes you through towering cane fields and secondary forest before reaching an older lava flow. The *Los Tucanes* trail connects to this, offering more forest immersion.
- **Wildlife**: You have a decent chance of spotting great curassows, toucans, and maybe a flash of a coati. However, because it gets very crowded, larger mammals tend to stay away from the main paths.
- **Expert Tip**: Include the short hike to the **Giant Ceiba Tree**—a massive, 400-year-old tree that survived the eruption.

**Is it for you?** Yes, if you are traveling with younger children, older adults, or prefer a mostly flat, easy walk.

## 3. Arenal Observatory Lodge: The Rainforest Immersion

Originally built for Smithsonian scientists to study the volcano, the **Arenal Observatory Lodge** sits on a ridge incredibly close to the giant. You don't have to be a guest to hike their extensive 11km trail network (you just pay a day pass).

- **The Vibe**: Deep jungle, waterfalls, and serious birdwatching.
- **The Trails**: Unlike the scrubby vegetation on the lava flows, the Observatory trails boast dense, mature rainforest. You can hike to the Danta Waterfall, cross hanging bridges, and climb their 28-meter observation tower.
- **Wildlife**: This is one of the premier birding spots in Costa Rica. Spider monkeys and howler monkeys are frequently spotted here.
- **Expert Tip**: Buy the day pass and plan to spend at least 4 hours here. Bring swimwear for the waterfall.

**Is it for you?** Yes, if you prioritize lush jungle, wildlife, and fewer crowds over walking directly on lava rock.
